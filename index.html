Independent project

Bacterial Growth Curves Analyser

BGCA is a web-based tool designed to support the analysis and interpretation of microbial growth experiments. It was developed in response to a practical problem I encountered while working with growth-curve data: different laboratories use different plate readers and export data in multiple file formats, while key analytical steps such as blank selection, curve smoothing, and log-phase detection are often handled in inconsistent ways. These differences can lead to conflicting interpretations. BGCA aims to make preliminary bacterial growth analysis more accessible, standardized, and better aligned with current literature recommendations. The project connects my background in experimental microbiology with a growing interest in computational biology and research-support software, and it is still in development.

  • Developed to address real challenges in analysing microbial growth-reader data across multiple devices and file formats.
  • Designed to support more consistent handling of steps such as blank correction, curve smoothing, and growth-phase interpretation.
  • Represents independent project development at the interface of biology, data analysis, and scientific software.

Details of work

  • Investigated how MaoP influences transcription of the ribosomal operons rrsB, rrsC, and rrsG in Escherichia coli by constructing chromosomal promoter–reporter fusions with mScarlet in wild-type, ΔmaoP, and ΔmaoS backgrounds.
  • Constructed and validated engineered bacterial strains using PCR, λ Red homologous recombination, electroporation, Flp/FRT cassette excision, diagnostic PCR, agarose gel electrophoresis, and sequencing.
  • Generated additional reporter strains by P1 phage transduction and prepared a complementation plasmid by PCR amplification, restriction digestion, ligation, bacterial transformation, and sequence verification.
  • Performed plate-reader fluorescence assays and analysed fluorescence data normalized to OD600 using custom Python scripts developed for the project to average replicates, calculate fluorescence-to-growth ratios, and generate summary statistics for plotting.
  • Showed that MaoP has a predominantly local effect on ribosomal operon expression, with the strongest effect observed for rrsC, the operon located closest to the maoS region.

Abstract

The bacterial chromosome is condensed and hierarchically organized – from nucleoid-associated proteins (NAPs), which bend, loop, or bundle DNA strands, through topological domains formed by DNA supercoiling, to macrodomains, within which DNA remains in close physical proximity and shows limited mobility compared to unstructured regions. The formation of macrodomains in Escherichia coli has been most thoroughly studied in the case of the Terminus Macrodomain, while the molecular details of other macrodomains remain less understood.

It has been shown that the MaoP protein, together with the maoS site, participates in restricting the mobility of the Origin Macrodomain. Preliminary results also suggest a link to the stringent response, due to MaoP's association with transcription of ribosomal operons and aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase genes, as well as the impairment of the replication block during this response. MaoP acts through regulation of Origin Macrodomain condensation and positioning.

This study describes the construction of E. coli strains carrying genetic fusions of ribosomal operon promoters with the gene encoding the mScarlet fluorescent protein, including strains with deletions of the maoP gene and of the maoS sequence together with the promoters of the surrounding maoP and hdfR genes. Strains were complemented with pGB2 plasmids carrying maoP or maoP together with hdfR. Fluorescence measurements were analysed in relation to the optical density of these cultures. MaoP was shown to locally suppress expression of the rrsC operon located closest to the maoS sequence.
