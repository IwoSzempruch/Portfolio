Abstract 
The bacterial chromosome is condensed and hierarchically organized – from nucleoid
associated proteins (NAPs), which bend, loop, or bundle DNA strands, through 
topological domains formed by DNA supercoiling, to macrodomains, within which DNA 
remains in close physical proximity and shows limited mobility compared to unstructured 
regions [1], [2]. The formation of Macrodomains in Escherichia coli has been most 
thoroughly studied in the case of the Terminus Macrodomain. Although the molecular 
details of its positioning and formation are still unclear, these processes are even less 
understood in other macrodomains [3]. It has been shown that the MaoP protein, together 
with the maoS site, participates in restricting the mobility of the Origin Macrodomain. 
Preliminary results also suggest a link to the stringent response, due to MaoP’s association 
with transcription of ribosomal operons and aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases genes, as well 
as the impairment of the replication block during this response. MaoP acts through 
regulation of Origin Macrodomain condensation and positioning. This study describes 
the construction of E. coli strains carrying genetic fusions of ribosomal operon promoters 
with the gene encoding the mScaret fluorescent protein, including strains with deletions 
of the maoP gene and of the maoS sequence together with the promoters of the 
surrounding maoP and hdfR genes. Strains have been complemented with pGB2 plasmids 
carrying maoP or maoP together with hdfR. Fluorescence measurements were analysed 
in relation to the optical density of these cultures. MaoP was shown to locally suppress 
expression of the rrsC operon located closest to the maoS sequence.
